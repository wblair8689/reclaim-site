How to free up space on a Mac when storage is full

When your Mac says its disk is almost full, apps slow down, updates fail and you can't save files. This checklist starts with what usually frees the most space for the least effort.

Updated November 14, 2026

Start here: find out what's actually big before deleting anything. Most of the time a handful of things (an old iPhone backup, a forgotten video project, Xcode data, a huge cache) account for most of the missing space.

1. See what's using your storage

Open System Settings › General › Storage. Click the ⓘ button next to each category to see its biggest items. If System Data is large, read what System Data is and how to shrink it.

2. Empty the Trash

Files you delete still use space until the Trash is emptied. Right-click the Trash in the Dock and choose Empty Trash. Also check Photos (Recently Deleted album) and Mail (Trash mailbox), which have their own.

3. Find large files

In Finder, press Command-F, set the first menu to Kind and change it to Other › File Size, then choose is greater than 500 MB. Old videos, disk images and virtual machines usually show up here.

4. Clean out Downloads

Sort ~/Downloads by size. Installer files (.dmg, .pkg, .xip) aren't needed once the app is installed. Anything you haven't opened in months is a good candidate too.

5. Delete old iPhone and iPad backups

Local backups made by Finder can be 10–100 GB each. In Finder, select your device in the sidebar and click Manage Backups, or open ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup. Delete backups of devices you no longer own, and older backups if you use iCloud Backup.

6. Remove apps you don't use

In Finder, open Applications, choose View › as List, and add the Date Last Opened column. Apps you haven't opened in a year are good candidates. Remember that dragging an app to the Trash leaves its data behind in ~/Library/Application Support and ~/Library/Containers.

7. Clear caches and logs

App caches in ~/Library/Caches and logs in ~/Library/Logs are safe to remove. See how to clear cache on a Mac safely.

8. If you code, clear developer data

Xcode DerivedData, simulators and package manager caches are often the single biggest win for developers. See how to delete Xcode DerivedData.

9. Store files in iCloud

In System Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Drive, turn on Optimize Mac Storage. Your Mac then keeps only recently used files locally and downloads others when you open them. For a single file, right-click it in iCloud Drive and choose Remove Download.

Photos has the same option: Photos › Settings › iCloud › Optimize Mac Storage.

10. Restart

A restart clears swap files and temporary data and lets macOS finish removing local Time Machine snapshots. It's common to see several gigabytes come back.

Before you delete: make sure you have a backup of anything you care about. Move files to the Trash rather than deleting them permanently, use your Mac for a day, and empty the Trash once you're sure nothing broke.

How much free space should a Mac have?

Try to keep at least 10–15% of your disk free, and 20–30 GB at minimum. macOS uses free space for virtual memory, caches and updates. Major macOS updates can need 20 GB or more to install.

Common questions

Why is my Mac storage full when I don't have many files?

Hidden data is usually the cause: caches, Time Machine local snapshots, old iPhone backups, Xcode data and leftovers from deleted apps. Check System Settings › General › Storage, especially System Data.

How much free space does a Mac need?

Aim for at least 10–15% of the disk, and 20–30 GB at minimum. Major macOS updates can need 20 GB or more to install.

Is it safe to delete files in the Library folder?

Caches and logs are safe. Application Support and Containers hold real app data, so only delete folders for apps you've removed. Never delete files in /System.
