How to clear cache on a Mac safely

Caches are files apps save so they don't have to download or calculate the same thing twice. Over time they can grow to many gigabytes. Here's how to clear them without causing problems.

Updated November 14, 2026

Short answer: Quit your apps, open ~/Library/Caches in Finder, and move the biggest folders to the Trash. Apps rebuild what they need. Skip system folders like /System/Library/Caches, and don't clear caches for sync services (like CloudKit) while they're syncing.

Is it safe to delete ~/Library/Caches?

For the most part, yes. Files in your user cache folder are, by design, data an app can recreate. Deleting them doesn't remove your documents, settings or passwords. The cost is that some apps feel slower the first time you open them afterward while they rebuild, and you may be signed out of a website or two.

There are a few exceptions, below.

How to clear app caches

  1. Quit the apps whose caches you want to clear. An app that's running can recreate files while you delete them or behave oddly.
  2. In Finder, choose Go › Go to Folder (Shift-Command-G), type ~/Library/Caches and press Return.
  3. Choose View › as List, then View › Show View Options and turn on Calculate all sizes. Click the Size column to sort.
  4. Select the large folders you want to clear and move them to the Trash (Command-Delete).
  5. Open your apps and check they work, then empty the Trash.

Folder names are usually the app's identifier, like com.spotify.client or com.google.Chrome, so it's easy to see what belongs to what.

Caches to leave alone

  • System caches in /System/Library/Caches and /Library/Caches. These belong to macOS, are mostly protected, and are small anyway.
  • Sync and account services such as CloudKit, com.apple.bird (iCloud Drive) and com.apple.HomeKit. Clearing these can force a long resync or cause sync glitches.
  • Caches of apps that are running. Quit first.

How to clear browser cache

Safari

Choose Safari › Settings › Advanced and turn on Show features for web developers. A Develop menu appears in the menu bar. Choose Develop › Empty Caches. This clears cached web files without signing you out of websites.

Chrome

Open chrome://settings/clearBrowserData, choose a time range, tick Cached images and files only, and click Delete data. Leave cookies unticked if you want to stay signed in.

Firefox

Open Settings › Privacy & Security › Cookies and Site Data › Clear Data, tick Cached Web Content only, and click Clear.

Other caches worth checking

  • Logs: ~/Library/Logs holds diagnostic logs and crash reports. Safe to clear unless you're troubleshooting something.
  • Developer caches: ~/.npm, ~/.gradle/caches, ~/.cargo/registry/cache and ~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData can be very large. See our Xcode guide.
  • Command-line tool caches: ~/.cache often holds downloaded AI models, which can be several gigabytes each. Safe to delete, but they're slow to download again.
  • Mail attachments: attachments you've opened in Mail are copied to ~/Library/Containers/com.apple.mail/Data/Library/Mail Downloads. The originals stay in your mailbox.

Will clearing cache speed up my Mac?

Usually not by much. Clearing caches frees disk space, and that matters a lot when your disk is nearly full: macOS needs free space for virtual memory and updates. But on a Mac with plenty of space, caches help apps run faster, so clearing them all on a schedule isn't worth it. Clear them when you need the space or when one app is misbehaving.

Common questions

Is it safe to delete everything in ~/Library/Caches?

Almost everything. Apps recreate their cache files. Quit apps first, and leave sync-related folders such as CloudKit and com.apple.bird alone to avoid forcing iCloud to resync.

Will I lose passwords or settings if I clear cache?

No. Passwords live in the keychain and settings live in ~/Library/Preferences. Clearing browser cookies, which is a separate option, is what signs you out of websites.

How often should I clear cache on a Mac?

Only when you need disk space or an app is misbehaving. Caches help apps run faster, so clearing them on a schedule isn't necessary.
