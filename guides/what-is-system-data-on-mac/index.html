What is System Data on a Mac, and how do you shrink it?

You open Storage settings and a grey bar labeled System Data is taking up 40, 80, even 150 GB. Here's what that bar contains and what you can do about it.

Updated November 14, 2026

Short answer: System Data is everything macOS can't put in another category: caches, logs, Time Machine snapshots, app support files, developer tools and virtual memory. Much of it is temporary. Clearing app caches, logs, Xcode build data and old local snapshots usually brings it down a lot. Don't delete anything inside /System or /private/var by hand.

Where to see it

Open System Settings › General › Storage. The bar at the top splits your disk into Applications, Documents, Photos, iCloud Drive, macOS and so on. System Data is the leftover bucket (older versions of macOS called it "Other"). Unlike the other categories, you can't click into it to see what's inside.

What's actually in System Data

Virtual memory and sleep image/private/var/vmNo. macOS manages these. A restart shrinks swap.
macOS's own files and updates/System, /LibraryNo. Leave them alone.

How to reduce System Data, step by step

1. Restart your Mac

It sounds too simple, but a restart clears swap files and some temporary data, and it lets macOS finish removing snapshots and leftover update files. Check Storage again afterward.

2. Clear app caches and logs

Quit your apps, then in Finder choose Go › Go to Folder (Shift-Command-G) and open ~/Library/Caches. Sort by size and move the biggest folders to the Trash. Do the same for ~/Library/Logs. Our cache guide covers which folders to skip.

3. Remove old Time Machine local snapshots

If you use Time Machine, your Mac keeps hourly snapshots on the internal disk while the backup drive is disconnected. To see them, open Terminal and run:

tmutil listlocalsnapshots /

To delete one, use the date from that list:

sudo tmutil deletelocalsnapshots 2026-11-10-093015

Connecting your backup drive and letting a backup finish also lets macOS clean these up on its own.

4. If you're a developer, clear Xcode and package caches

Xcode's DerivedData, device support files and old simulators often add up to 20–100 GB. They're safe to delete and Xcode rebuilds what it needs. See how to delete Xcode DerivedData.

5. Remove leftovers from apps you've deleted

Dragging an app to the Trash leaves its data behind in ~/Library/Application Support and ~/Library/Containers. Folders are usually named after the app or its developer (for example com.vendor.appname). If the app is gone and you won't reinstall it, those folders can go too.

Don't delete anything inside /System, /private/var or /Library/Apple. Those are part of macOS, are mostly protected anyway, and deleting what isn't protected can stop your Mac from starting up.

Why System Data comes back

Caches and logs grow again as you use your Mac, and that's normal. The goal isn't to get System Data to zero, it's to get rid of the large, stale parts: a 30 GB cache from an app you stopped using, years of Xcode builds, or backups of an iPhone you no longer own.

Common questions

Is it safe to delete System Data on a Mac?

Parts of it are. App caches, logs, Time Machine local snapshots and Xcode build data are safe to remove because they are rebuilt or not needed. Files inside /System and /private/var belong to macOS and should be left alone.

Why is my System Data so large?

Common causes are large app caches, Time Machine local snapshots while your backup drive is disconnected, Xcode and simulator data, and data left behind by apps you deleted.

Does restarting a Mac reduce System Data?

Often, yes. A restart clears swap files and some temporary data and gives macOS a chance to remove snapshots and leftover update files.
